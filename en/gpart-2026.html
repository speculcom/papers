Adaptation

GPart: End-to-End Isometric Fine-Tuning via Global Parameter Partitioning

Problem: LoRA’s bilinear parameterization does not preserve distance between trainable parameters and weight updates; projecting a low-dimensional vector into LoRA’s parameter space still breaks end-to-end isometry through the subsequent bilinear map.
Where the evidence sits: the abstract says GPart maps a d-dimensional trainable vector directly into the full weight space through a sparse, isometric partition matrix; it has one main hyperparameter d, and the checkpoint is just that vector plus a random seed; on natural-language-understanding, computer-vision and mathematical-reasoning benchmarks it matches or improves existing PEFT methods at ultra-low parameter budgets.
What the authors say about limits: still a preprint (v3); the abstract only says “match or improve”, without absolute numbers or a per-task table; choosing d still needs experiments; large-scale distributed training and production serving are not reported.
What it does not claim: a general replacement for LoRA; validation at trillion-parameter scale; a training-speed or throughput result; no tuning required.
