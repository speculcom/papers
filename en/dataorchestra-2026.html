DataOrchestra: Learning to Orchestrate Per-Example Curation of Pretraining Data

Problem: pretraining data processing usually fixes one strategy at corpus or domain level and applies it uniformly; different examples need different treatment.
Where the evidence sits: an orchestrator decides per chunk whether to drop, keep or clean; when cleaning it selects one or more downstream operations from programmatic editing to LLM rewriting and generates a concrete instruction for each step; models from 0.5B to 7B pretrained from scratch show stable average gains over single processing methods across 11 benchmarks, also on maths continued pretraining, while skipping unnecessary operations reduces processing compute.
What the authors say about limits: training the orchestrator and tool models adds upfront engineering cost; 0.5B–7B scale, not validated at hundred-billion scale; 11 benchmarks lean general and maths, with limited code/multilingual coverage; gains depend on tool-model quality.
What it does not claim: that more data is always better; a fixed rule set; a replacement for human data audit.
