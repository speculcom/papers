Six papers, six “what it does not claim” notes

Edit-based training supervises only the first failure; high-stakes target formulation checks decision dependency; Byteification converts subword models to bytes with 49.1B tokens; JEPA-PRH tests cross-modal alignment at 109M; recognition–refusal explains “knowing it is unanswerable yet answering” as routing failure; Abstain-R1 does calibrated abstention at 3B.

Only entries with text-level evidence; each states the problem, evidence location, author-stated limits and what it does not claim. Preprints and peer review are labelled separately.

2026-11-19arXiv / training

Edit-based training: supervise only the first failure

Instead of imitating a full reasoning trajectory or rewarding the whole completion, an editor locates the first point of failure and supervises only a minimal replacement span. The method works offline or online, with supervision moving to the model’s current failure modes. The authors test it on Qwen3-32B with ReasonIF.

arXiv
2611.26342
model
Qwen3-32B
benchmark
ReasonIF
venue
NeurIPS 2026 workshop
reproduction
未复现
Where the evidence sits

The abstract reports that, versus full-trajectory imitation and trajectory-level RL, edit-based training substantially improves compliance with reasoning instructions while largely preserving untargeted task skills; supervision concentrates on the minimal replacement span after the failure point.

Boundary / what it does not claim

Only Qwen3-32B and one benchmark, ReasonIF, are tested; this does not generalise to all models or tasks. The “first failure” depends on the editor and criterion, and edit quality caps the gain; the paper gives no production-scale or cross-lingual results. It does not claim to replace RLHF or to work for every reasoning task.

Sources：arXiv

2026-11-18arXiv / high-stakes decisions

Do LLMs formulate the right prediction target?

The paper asks whether LLMs correctly formulate prediction problems in child welfare, healthcare and employment, focusing on decision dependency: past decisions shape observed outcomes. It tests 11 OpenAI / Anthropic models under seven prompt conditions, checking whether generated code and explanations handle that bias.

arXiv
2611.23638
models
11 个 OpenAI / Anthropic 模型
prompts
7 种提示
domains
儿童福利 / 医疗 / 就业
reproduction
未复现
Where the evidence sits

Abstract: without guidance, LLMs do not reliably address decision dependency; when the prompt specifies the target, the required assumption and the estimation procedure, target-aligned implementation reaches 99–100%. Among frontier models, a simple description of how decisions affect outcomes appears sufficient.

Boundary / what it does not claim

The tests cover three high-stakes domains and specific prompts, not all data analysis; 99–100% is the share of target-aligned implementations, not predictive accuracy or deployment outcomes; some answers acknowledge decision dependency in prose while failing to handle it in code. It does not claim LLMs can automate high-stakes modelling, or that prompting replaces methodological review.

Sources：arXiv

2026-10-29arXiv / representation

JEPA-PRH: cross-modal alignment from a 109M text JEPA

A 109M-parameter text JEPA aligns with image encoders better than every language model the authors evaluated, up to 72B; “good views” need 42 times fewer training samples than next-token prediction.

arXiv
2610.39302
authors
Balestriero & LeCun
model
109M text JEPA
reproduction
未复现
Where the evidence sits

The abstract reports alignment with DINOv2 / CLIP and the 42× sample efficiency; the body uses JEPA as a provable/improveable instance.

Boundary / what it does not claim

A preprint; 109M is far smaller than the compared LLMs; alignment is not generation quality; 42× is specific to views and data, not a universal constant. It does not claim small models beat large models across the board, or that PRH is finally proved.

Sources：arXiv

2026-10-07Nature / architecture

Byteification: retrofitting subword models to bytes

Converts an existing subword LLM to byte level with 49.1B tokens, less than 1% of a typical pretraining budget; reports Bolmo 7B/1B, Bwen 8B and Blama 8B beating earlier byte-level approaches on character-level reasoning.

DOI
10.1038/s41586-026-11111-4
budget
49.1B tokens（<1%）
models
Bolmo 7B/1B、Bwen 8B、Blama 8B
reproduction
未复现
Where the evidence sits

The abstract gives the method and the 49.1B-token conversion cost; the “Tokenizer transfer and retrofitting” section describes the two-stage procedure; the results report character-level tasks and practical inference speed.

Boundary / what it does not claim

49.1B is under 1% but still large in absolute terms; the method needs an existing subword teacher; byte-level inference needs dedicated kernels; the paper does not cover every task. It does not claim byte-level beats subword everywhere, or that tokenisers are obsolete.

Sources：Nature / DOI

2026EMNLP / reasoning

Recognition–refusal misalignment: knowing it is unanswerable, yet answering

The paper finds a linear direction in hidden states separating answerable from structurally impossible math/code prompts; recognition is nearly orthogonal to safety refusal, so the failure is a routing problem, not an encoding problem.

venue
EMNLP 2026 Main
anthology ID
2026.emnlp-main.1078
tasks
数学 / 代码的结构性不可答
reproduction
未复现
Where the evidence sits

A linear probe separates the two classes; the recognition and refusal directions are nearly orthogonal.

Boundary / what it does not claim

It covers structurally unanswerable math/code, not every “don’t know”; linear separability does not prove the model uses that direction when generating; no general fix is offered. It does not claim every hallucination is routing, or that a refusal head solves it.

Sources：ACL Anthology

2026ACL / alignment

Abstain-R1: calibrated abstention at 3B

Trains a 3B model with a clarification-aware RLVR reward, improving abstention and clarification on unanswerable queries while preserving answerable-query performance.

arXiv
2604.17073
venue
ACL 2026
scale
3B
reproduction
未复现
Where the evidence sits

The abstract reports gains in abstention/clarification on unanswerable queries and preserved performance on answerable ones; the method is verifiable-reward RL.

Boundary / what it does not claim

3B scale, not extrapolable to 70B+; tasks centre on unanswerable queries, with limited open-domain multi-turn coverage; rewards depend on verifiable labels that carry noise; no production validation. It does not claim more abstention is always better, or that all hallucinations are solved.

Sources：arXiv
