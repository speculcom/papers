High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models

Problem: pixel-space diffusion is high quality but costs hundreds of GPU days to train and repeatedly runs a full-resolution network at inference; can images be compressed into a latent space and diffused there to buy efficiency?
Where the evidence sits: method in Section 3, splitting perceptual from semantic compression (3.1), latent diffusion (3.2), and cross-attention conditioning (3.3); experiments in Section 4 (unconditional, layout/class/text conditioning, super-resolution, inpainting); limitations in Section 5 and conclusion in Section 6.
Stated limits: Section 5 lists two — latent diffusion still samples sequentially and is slower than GANs; and although the f=4 autoencoder loses very little quality, its reconstruction can bottleneck tasks needing fine-grained pixel accuracy, with super-resolution called out as already limited.
What it does not claim: it does not claim latent compression is lossless — Section 5 says high-precision settings may not suffice; it does not claim LDMs beat pixel diffusion on every task — the abstract calls text-to-image, unconditional generation and super-resolution “competitive”; and it does not claim cross-attention understands arbitrary conditions, since experiments cover class, text and layout but not open-ended control.
