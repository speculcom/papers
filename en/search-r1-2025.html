Search-R1: Training LLMs to Reason and Leverage Search Engines with Reinforcement Learning

Problem: RAG usually injects retrieved results into a prompt once, leaving the model unable to decide when to search, what to search and how often; can RL teach it to issue queries during step-by-step reasoning.
Where the evidence sits: Search-R1 autonomously generates multiple search queries with real-time retrieval during reasoning; retrieved-token masking stabilises RL and a simple outcome reward is used; across seven QA datasets it improves over several RAG baselines by 41% for Qwen2.5-7B and 20% for Qwen2.5-3B; code and checkpoints are open.
What the authors say about limits: only 3B/7B models; tasks are seven QA datasets; the retrieval environment and search tool are fixed; open-domain research, multi-hop browsing and production latency are not covered.
What it does not claim: not that every RAG system should switch to RL; not that 41% is a uniform gain across tasks; not that more searches are always better.
