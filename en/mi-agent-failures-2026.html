Can Mechanistic Interpretability Help Explain LLM Agent Failures? A Case Study on Web Element Hallucination

Problem: MI has progressed in explaining internal LLM computation but has not demonstrated comparable success in explaining LLM-agent decision failures.
Where the evidence sits: the case study targets web agents hallucinating unavailable actions, applying mechanistic analyses to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on live websites; a linear probe on the last prompt token predicts hallucination before generation (0.80 AUC), and steering along the probe direction corrects 85% of hallucinations while disrupting 7% of valid actions.
What the authors say about limits: the probe direction is sensitive to prompt formatting and loses most steering effect after a minor change in action representation; circuit identification is difficult because computation is spread across components and positions and the relied-on information varies by example; one 7B model and one web setting; 0.80 AUC/85% are offline probe results, not a production real-time guard.
What it does not claim: a general agent-failure explainer; that probes solve hallucination; that circuit methods are mature for agents.
